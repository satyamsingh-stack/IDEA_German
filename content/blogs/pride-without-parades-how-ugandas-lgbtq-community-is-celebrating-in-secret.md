title: "Pride Without Parades: How Uganda's LGBTQ+ Community Is Celebrating in Secret"
description: >
  While much of the world celebrates Pride Month with colorful parades,
  festivals, and public events, Uganda's LGBTQ+ community faces a very different
  reality. Public Pride celebrations have been effectively shut down for years
  due to police crackdowns, arrests, and increasingly strict anti-LGBTQ laws.
  Instead of giving up, many Ugandan LGBTQ+ people have found new ways to
  celebrate. This year, activists organized a digital Pride campaign called
  "Pride in Our Words," where community members shared personal stories of
  resilience, hope, and identity online rather than gathering in public. The
  stories reveal a common theme: for many queer Ugandans, Pride is no longer
  about parades or parties. It is about survival. Community members describe
  Pride as the courage to live authentically despite rejection, discrimination,
  and fear, while finding strength through chosen family, friendship, and mutual
  support. Although Uganda remains one of the world's most difficult places to
  be openly LGBTQ+, these voices show that Pride cannot be erased. Even when
  public celebrations are impossible, the spirit of Pride continues through
  resilience, solidarity, and the determination to keep living authentically.
  Sometimes, the most powerful act of Pride isn't marching in the streets—it's
  simply refusing to disappear.
