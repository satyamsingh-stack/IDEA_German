title: "Surrogacy in Switzerland: A Debate Returns"
description: 
The recent controversy surrounding German politician Jens Spahn has revived
the debate on surrogacy in both Germany and Switzerland. A similar case
occurred in Switzerland in 2017, when a Centre Party politician became a
father through a surrogate mother in the United States. Unlike the current
debate in Germany, the public reaction in Switzerland was relatively moderate.
The politician argues that society is often more open to surrogacy than
politics. Supporters of legalisation believe that surrogacy already happens abroad 
and that allowing it in Switzerland would provide better legal oversight and stronger 
protection for surrogate mothers, intended parents, and children. 
Surrogacy also enables both same-sex and heterosexual couples facing infertility to 
have children. Critics, however, warn of the risks of exploitation, health complications 
for surrogate mothers, and ethical concerns about the welfare and rights of children, including 
their right to know their biological origins. At present, surrogacy remains prohibited 
in Switzerland and is even banned under the Swiss Constitution. Although the government 
is considering reforms such as legalising egg donation, there are currently no plans to 
legalise surrogacy. The debate ultimately reflects the challenge of balancing the 
desire to become parents with the protection of human dignity, children's rights, 
and ethical standards.
