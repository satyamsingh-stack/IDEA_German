title: Oklahoma Expands Restrictions on Trans Healthcare
description: Oklahoma has become one of a growing number of U.S. states
  restricting access to gender-affirming healthcare. In May, lawmakers passed
  law, extending restrictions beyond minors to also affect adults by barring
  Medicaid funding for gender-affirming treatments and prohibiting such care on
  public property. Although the law officially takes effect on November 1, its
  emergency provisions have already disrupted care. Since June, Oklahoma's
  Medicaid program has denied claims related to gender dysphoria treatment,
  while advocates report that some patients have already experienced
  interruptions in care. Supporters of the law argue it is about limiting the
  use of taxpayer-funded healthcare, while critics say it places political
  ideology above medical judgment and disproportionately harms transgender
  people, particularly those facing financial hardship. Major medical
  organizations continue to recognize gender-affirming care as an evidence-based
  treatment for gender dysphoria. Oklahoma is now among a dozen states
  restricting Medicaid coverage for gender-affirming care for adults. The move
  follows the U.S. Supreme Court's decision in United States vs Skrmetti, which
  upheld state restrictions on gender-affirming care for minors, prompting
  concerns that similar policies could increasingly target adults. Advocates
  also warn that public insurance restrictions may influence private insurers to
  reduce coverage, further limiting healthcare access. In response, some federal
  lawmakers have proposed legislation to strengthen protections for transgender
  healthcare, while several cities have expanded local support for trans health
  services. The debate over gender-affirming care remains one of the most
  significant civil rights and healthcare issues in the United States, with
  legal, political, and medical battles continuing across the country.
