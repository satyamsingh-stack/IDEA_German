## IDEA Legal Research Paper Series - Vol. 3

### Abstract

This paper reviews recent jurisprudence from the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR), national courts, and administrative tribunals addressing asylum claims based on persecution related to sexual orientation and gender identity (SOGI).

### Scope

The review covers:
- ECtHR landmark decisions
- National case law from selected European jurisdictions
- Administrative tribunal decisions
- Emerging standards and best practices

### Legal Framework

The analysis is grounded in:
- Article 3 ECHR (prohibition of torture and degrading treatment)
- Article 8 ECHR (right to private and family life)
- Article 14 ECHR (prohibition of discrimination)
- 1951 Refugee Convention

### Key Holdings

**ECtHR Standard**  
The ECtHR has established that persecution based on sexual orientation and gender identity can constitute grounds for asylum protection.

**Evidence and Credibility**  
Applicants need not provide explicit external evidence of persecution; testimony can be sufficient when credible.

**Discrimination as Persecution**  
Systemic discrimination, when severe and enduring, can constitute persecution even absent direct state action.

### Analysis of Trends

1. **Expansion of protected categories** - increasing recognition of gender identity as distinct from sexual orientation
2. **Procedural improvements** - adoption of specialized procedures and training
3. **Regional variation** - significant differences in application across jurisdictions

### Recommendations

- Adopt explicit legal standards recognizing SOGI persecution
- Implement specialized training for adjudicators
- Ensure adequate legal representation
- Develop procedures responsive to trauma
- Monitor implementation for consistency
